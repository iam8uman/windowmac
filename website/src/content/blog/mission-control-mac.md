Mission Control shows every open window on your Mac spread out in one layer, with your desktop spaces and full-screen apps as thumbnails along the top. Open it with **Control-Up Arrow**, the **Mission Control key**, or a **three-finger swipe up** on the trackpad, then click any window to jump to it. Press the same key or swipe down to leave.

That is the core of it. Below is everything else Mission Control can do, plus the settings that stop it behaving unexpectedly.

## How do I open Mission Control?

| Device | Open | Leave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard | <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>↑</kbd> or the Mission Control key | Same again |
| Trackpad | Swipe up with three fingers (four on some setups) | Swipe down |
| Magic Mouse | Double-tap with two fingers | Double-tap again |
| Mouse or trackpad | Drag a window to the top of the screen (if the setting is on) | Click the desktop |

The trackpad gesture can be changed or turned off in **System Settings → Trackpad**, and the keyboard shortcut in **Keyboard** settings. Apple notes that on a second display, Mission Control shows only that display's windows and spaces.

## What does Mission Control show?

- **The middle of the screen:** every open window on the current desktop, laid out so nothing overlaps. Hover over one and click to bring it to the front.
- **The Spaces bar along the top:** a thumbnail for each desktop space, each full-screen app and each Split View pair. Move the pointer to the top edge to expand it.

If a window you expect is missing, check whether it is minimised in the Dock or its app is hidden with Command-H.

## How do I see only one app's windows?

Press <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>↓</kbd>. This view, called App Exposé, shows only the windows of the app you are in, which is handy when you have five browser windows and want the right one. With App Exposé enabled in Trackpad settings, you can also swipe down with three or four fingers. Press the keys again or swipe up to go back.

If you want Mission Control itself to cluster windows by app, turn on **Group windows by application** in **Desktop & Dock** settings.

## How do I show the desktop?

Press <kbd>Command</kbd>-Mission Control, <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>H</kbd> or <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>F11</kbd>, or spread your thumb and three fingers apart on the trackpad. All windows slide aside to reveal the desktop. Do it again, or pinch, to bring them back. This is also the quickest way to drop a file onto the desktop: start dragging it, show the desktop, then let go.

## How do I move a window to another desktop?

1. Open Mission Control.
2. Drag the window up to a space thumbnail in the Spaces bar.
3. Let go. The window moves to that space.

To add a new space, click the **Add (+)** button at the right end of the Spaces bar. Drop a window onto a full-screen app's thumbnail instead and you create a Split View with the two apps. There is a lot more to spaces, including assigning apps to them, in our guide to [using multiple desktops on a Mac](/blog/multiple-desktops-mac/).

## Which Mission Control settings should I change?

Open **System Settings → Desktop & Dock** and scroll to **Mission Control**.

| Setting | What it does | Suggestion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Automatically rearrange Spaces based on most recent use** | Moves recently used spaces first | Turn off if your spaces keep shuffling |
| **When switching to an application, switch to a Space with open windows for the application** | Jumps to the space where that app is | Keep on if you sort apps by space |
| **Group windows by application** | Clusters windows by app in Mission Control | Turn on if you have many windows per app |
| **Displays have separate Spaces** | Each display gets its own spaces | Needed for Stage Manager and Split View on other displays |
| **Drag windows to top of screen to enter Mission Control** | Opens Mission Control when you drag to the top | Turn off if you drag to the menu bar to fill the screen |

The **Shortcuts** button on the same page lets you change the keys for Mission Control, application windows and Show Desktop. **Hot Corners** lets you open Mission Control by pushing the pointer into a corner.

## When is Mission Control the wrong tool?

Mission Control is excellent for finding a window. It is less good for arranging them, because it does not change where windows sit or how big they are. If you open Mission Control mostly to dig out the windows you need and then drag them side by side, snapping is quicker: see [how to snap windows on a Mac](/blog/how-to-snap-windows-on-mac/). A window manager such as [Windowmac](/) can also line up every window on a screen as scrolling columns with its Strip mode (hold Option and press S), which keeps them all visible without overlapping.

| You want to | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Find one window among many | Mission Control or App Exposé |
| Clear the screen for a moment | Show Desktop |
| Keep work and personal apps apart | Desktop spaces |
| Keep a few apps visible side by side | Tiling or a window manager |
