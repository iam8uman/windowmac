The best Rectangle alternatives for Mac are **Windowmac** or **Loop** if you want a free, open-source gesture instead of dragging to screen edges, **Rectangle Pro** or **Moom** if you want saved multi-app layouts, **Magnet** if you prefer a cheap Mac App Store app, **Raycast** if you already use it as a launcher, and **AeroSpace** if you want windows tiled automatically. If Rectangle does what you need, there is no reason to leave: it is free, open source and still being updated.

Here is why people move on from Rectangle, then each option with verified facts.

## Why would you look for a Rectangle alternative?

Rectangle is a keyboard-and-snap-areas window manager based on the older Spectacle app. People usually look elsewhere for one of these reasons:

- **macOS version.** The current Rectangle needs macOS 14 or later. Older builds exist for earlier systems, but they no longer get new features.
- **Aiming at edges.** Snap areas mean dragging a window to the screen edge, which is slow on large or ultrawide monitors.
- **Too many shortcuts to remember.** Rectangle has dozens of keyboard actions; some people want fewer, more visual controls.
- **Multi-app layouts.** Rectangle arranges one window at a time.
- **Automatic tiling.** Rectangle moves windows only when asked.

## 1. Windowmac: a gesture under your pointer

[Windowmac](/) replaces edge snapping with a radial gesture. Hold **Option**, nudge the mouse toward where the window should go, and let go; a ring under the pointer shows the target and an outline previews the result. Click while holding to cycle half, third and two thirds. Holding Option and pressing a key works too: arrows for halves and thirds, 1 to 4 for quarters, brackets for the next or previous screen, Z to undo.

It also has **Strip**, which lines up every window on a screen as scrolling columns, plus stash, custom layouts and a `windowmac://` link for every action. It is free and MIT licensed, needs only the Accessibility permission and never connects to the internet. The trade-off: it is not on the Mac App Store, so you install it from its website with a one-line command or a .zip download.

**Best for:** big monitors, mouse users, and anyone on macOS 13 who wants a current, free app.

## 2. Loop: the original radial window manager

Loop is a free, open-source (GPL-3.0) window manager and the radial app that inspired Windowmac: hold a trigger key, move toward a direction, release. It has a preview, keybinds paired with the trigger key, cycles, stash, theming, and control through a URL scheme, shell commands or AppleScript. It needs macOS 13 or later and installs with `brew install loop`.

**Best for:** people who want a radial menu with AppleScript and shell control. Our [Windowmac vs Loop](/blog/windowmac-vs-loop/) comparison covers the differences.

## 3. Rectangle Pro: Rectangle with layouts

Made by Rectangle's developer and built on top of it. Rectangle Pro adds app and workspace layouts (which can trigger when displays connect), custom snap areas, custom sizes, stash, pinning an app to a side, a configurable menu, and iCloud sync. It has a 10-day trial and a license works on three Macs. Its site did not show a price when we checked, so look there for the current one.

**Best for:** Rectangle users who want saved layouts without learning a new app.

## 4. Magnet: the inexpensive App Store pick

Magnet costs $4.99 on the US Mac App Store, with no subscription and no in-app purchases. It snaps by dragging to trigger areas, by keyboard shortcuts, from the green button menu or from the menu bar, supports external and vertical displays, and syncs through iCloud. The listing requires macOS 13.0 or later.

**Best for:** people who want an App Store purchase and a familiar Rectangle-like feel. See [Magnet vs Rectangle](/blog/magnet-vs-rectangle/) for a head-to-head.

## 5. Moom: palette, grids and layouts

Moom shows a palette when you hover over a window's green button, with up to 61 actions including grids and saved layouts. Layouts can be tied to specific apps or applied to your most recent windows, and keyboard mode lets you move and resize without the mouse. Moom 4 is $15 with a free trial; Moom Classic is on the Mac App Store.

**Best for:** people who rebuild the same multi-window arrangement every day.

## 6. Raycast: window management inside a launcher

Raycast's Window Management covers halves, thirds, quarters, maximise, centre, next and previous display and restore, each assignable to a hotkey. It is included in the free plan; custom window management commands are part of Raycast Pro, which costs $10 a month or $96 a year.

**Best for:** people who already use Raycast and only need keyboard snapping.

## 7. AeroSpace: automatic tiling

AeroSpace is a free, MIT-licensed, i3-like tiling window manager. It arranges windows in a tree as they open, uses its own virtual workspaces instead of macOS Spaces, is configured with a plain-text file and does not require disabling System Integrity Protection. It is in public beta and not notarized.

**Best for:** keyboard-first developers who want every window tiled. Compare it with yabai in [yabai vs AeroSpace](/blog/yabai-vs-aerospace/).

## Do you need an alternative at all?

If you are on macOS Sequoia 15 or later and mostly use halves and fill, the built-in tiling may be enough; our [macOS tiling vs Rectangle](/blog/macos-tiling-vs-rectangle/) guide compares them directly.
