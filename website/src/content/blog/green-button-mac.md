Clicking the green button in a Mac window's top-left corner makes the app **full screen** in a space of its own, hiding the menu bar and Dock. **Hovering** over it, without clicking, opens a menu with tiling layouts (halves, quarters, fill and multi-window arrangements), full-screen and Split View options. Holding **Option** shows extra layouts, and Option-clicking puts a filled window back to its previous size.

It is the most misunderstood control on the Mac, especially for people used to Windows, where the third button maximises. Here is everything it does.

## What happens when I click the green button?

The window goes **full screen**. macOS moves the app into a new space, hides the menu bar and Dock until you move the pointer to them, and fills the whole display. Click the button again (move the pointer to the top to reveal it), press <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>F</kbd>, or choose **Exit Full Screen** from its menu to leave. Our guide to [exiting full screen](/blog/exit-full-screen-mac/) covers the other ways out.

## What is in the green button menu?

Hover over the button for a moment and a menu appears. On macOS Sequoia 15 and later it has these sections:

| Section | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Move & Resize** | Tiles this window to a half or a quarter of the desktop |
| **Fill & Arrange** | Fills the desktop with this window, or arranges two or more recent windows in a layout |
| **Full Screen** | Entire Screen, or Left of Screen / Right of Screen to start Split View |

Hold **Option** while the menu is open to see more layouts. Apple's tiling guide also says that pressing Option and clicking the centred tile icon under Fill & Arrange centres the window.

On macOS Ventura and Sonoma the menu is shorter. Apple's guide for those versions describes options such as tiling the window, which there means starting Split View; the Move & Resize and Fill & Arrange sections came with Sequoia.

## What extra options appear in Split View?

When an app is in Split View, the menu adds:

- **Replace Tiled Window**: swap the app on that side for another window.
- **Move Window to Desktop**: take this app out of Split View and back to your normal desktop.
- **Make Window Full Screen**: give this app its own full-screen space; the other app stays full screen in its space.

See [how to split screen on a Mac](/blog/how-to-split-screen-on-mac/) for Split View in full.

## How do I make the green button maximise?

Three ways to get a Windows-style maximise:

1. Hover and choose the fill option under **Fill & Arrange**, or press <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>F</kbd> (macOS 15+).
2. Set **double-clicking a window's title bar** to **Fill** in **Desktop & Dock** settings and double-click the title bar instead.
3. On macOS Ventura or Sonoma, **Option-click** the green button. Apple's guide for those versions says this maximises the window instead of making it full screen.

Option-click also works the other way on newer versions: Apple notes it returns a filled window to its previous size. More detail is in [how to maximise without full screen](/blog/maximize-window-mac-without-full-screen/).

## Why is the green button greyed out?

The window cannot be resized or made full screen. Apple notes that some windows cannot be resized; settings panels and dialogs are the usual examples. Nothing you change in settings will enable it.

## Do I have to use the green button at all?

No. Everything in its menu is also under **Window** in the menu bar, and most of it has a keyboard shortcut. Window managers replace it entirely for many people. With [Windowmac](/), you hold Option and flick the mouse toward where the window should go, so you never need to aim at a small button in the corner; the ring appears right under the pointer.

| You want | Instead of the green button |
| --- | --- |
| Full screen | Control-Command-F |
| Fill the desktop | Fn-Control-F |
| Left or right half | Fn-Control-Left or Right Arrow |
| Thirds, next screen | A window manager |
