To auto-hide the menu bar on a Mac, open **System Settings → Menu Bar** and set **Automatically hide and show the menu bar** to **Always** or **On Desktop Only**. The menu bar then disappears until you move the pointer to the top of the screen. Choose **Never** to keep it visible all the time, including in full-screen apps.

## Where is the setting?

1. Choose **Apple menu → System Settings**.
2. Click **Menu Bar** in the sidebar (you may need to scroll).
3. Open **Automatically hide and show the menu bar** and pick an option.

On older macOS versions the setting lived in **Dock & Menu Bar** or **Control Center** settings, but it does the same job.

## What do the four options do?

| Option | Desktop | Full-screen apps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Always** | Hidden | Hidden |
| **On Desktop Only** | Hidden | Shown |
| **In Full Screen Only** | Shown | Hidden |
| **Never** | Shown | Shown |

In every case, moving the pointer to the top edge reveals a hidden menu bar.

**On Desktop Only** sounds odd but has a use: in full screen, there is nothing else on screen to show which app you are in, so some people like the menu bar visible there.

## How does hiding the menu bar affect windows?

When the menu bar is shown, filled and tiled windows start below it. Hide it, and windows can use the extra strip at the top. The gain is small on a large monitor and more noticeable on a laptop, especially on MacBooks with a notch, where the menu bar is taller.

The [window size calculator](/tools/window-size-calculator/) has a menu bar field: set it to zero to see how big halves and quarters get with the menu bar hidden.

## What are the downsides?

- **You lose the clock, battery and status icons** at a glance. Moving the pointer up shows them, but that is an extra step.
- **Menus take a moment longer** to reach, since the bar has to slide in.
- **Apps that put useful status in the menu bar**, such as timers or system monitors, are hidden too.

If the clutter of icons is what bothers you, consider hiding some items instead: the **Menu Bar Controls** and **Allow in the Menu Bar** sections on the same settings page decide which items appear.

## Should I hide the Dock too?

If you want the most space for windows, hide both. The Dock has its own setting, **Automatically hide and show the Dock**, in **Desktop & Dock**, and the shortcut <kbd>Option</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>D</kbd> toggles it. See [how to auto-hide the Dock](/blog/auto-hide-dock-mac/).

## What about full screen instead?

Full screen hides both bars automatically but moves the app into its own space. For most work, filling the desktop is the better middle ground: the window gets almost all the space while you keep access to other apps. [How to maximise without full screen](/blog/maximize-window-mac-without-full-screen/) covers it, and [Windowmac](/) does it with a flick upward while holding Option, filling exactly the space between whatever bars you have left visible.

## Which option should I pick?

| You | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Use a small laptop screen and want space | Always |
| Want the clock visible on the desktop | Never or In Full Screen Only |
| Use full-screen apps and want menus there | On Desktop Only or Never |
| Have a large monitor | Never; the space saved is small |
