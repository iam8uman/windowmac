To set up a MacBook with an external monitor, connect the monitor to a Thunderbolt, USB-C or HDMI port, then open **System Settings → Displays**. Set **Use as** to **Extended display** to get a second screen, click **Arrange** to place it where it sits on your desk, and choose a resolution that makes text a comfortable size. Then decide which screen gets the menu bar and how to split your apps between them.

## How do I connect the monitor?

1. Check your ports. Apple's guide lists USB-C, Thunderbolt and HDMI among the video ports Macs use. Use the monitor's cable if it fits, or an adapter if not.
2. Check how many displays your MacBook supports: open **System Settings**, choose **Help** in the menu bar, open your Mac's **Specifications** and look under **Display Support** or **Video Support**.
3. Connect the monitor and turn it on. The MacBook should detect it automatically.

If nothing shows up, open **Displays**, hold **Option** and click **Detect Displays**, check the cable, or put the Mac to sleep and wake it.

## Extend or mirror?

In **Displays**, select the monitor and open **Use as**:

| Option | What you get | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extended display | Two separate screens, more space | Everyday work |
| Mirror | The same picture on both | Presenting |
| Main display | Extended, with the menu bar and Dock on this screen | Docked at a desk |

## Which screen should be the main display?

The main display is the one that holds the menu bar. When the laptop sits beside a larger monitor, make the monitor the main display: select it and set **Use as** to **Main display**, or click **Arrange** and drag the menu bar onto it.

If **Displays have separate Spaces** is on (in **Desktop & Dock**, under Mission Control), each screen gets its own spaces and the Dock can appear on either display. Apple notes this setting is also needed for Stage Manager.

## How do I line the screens up?

1. In **Displays**, click **Arrange**.
2. Drag the display icons to match your desk: side by side, or the monitor above the laptop.
3. Line up the edges so the pointer crosses where you expect.

A MacBook screen shows more points per inch than most monitors, so the laptop's icon looks smaller in Arrange than it looks on your desk. The [multi-monitor planner](/tools/multi-monitor-planner/) shows both views and how far to slide one display to align the centres.

## What resolution should the monitor use?

Pick the resolution that makes text a similar size on both screens. Apple's Displays settings let you choose Default or other scaled sizes, and **Show all resolutions** reveals more. A 27-inch 4K monitor, for example, can be comfortable looking like 2560 by 1440, while a 27-inch 1440p monitor is used at its native 2560 by 1440. The [display scaling calculator](/tools/display-scaling-calculator/) compares text size on any monitor with a MacBook screen.

## How should I split my work across the two screens?

Common patterns:

| Laptop screen | Monitor |
| --- | --- |
| Chat, email, music | Main work, split into halves or thirds |
| Reference or video call | Editor or design app full height |
| Notes | Browser and documents side by side |

Moving windows between the screens is the habit to get fast at. Dragging works; a window manager is quicker. [Windowmac](/) sends the active window to the next screen with Option-] and keeps its layout, so a left half stays a left half, and the pointer comes along. See [how to move a window to another monitor](/blog/move-window-to-another-monitor-mac/) for every method.

## What about the laptop lid?

You can keep the MacBook open beside the monitor and use both screens, which is the setup this guide assumes. If you would rather use only the monitor, check Apple's guidance for your model before closing the lid, and make sure the Mac has power and an external keyboard and mouse or trackpad. With the lid open, you can still make the monitor the main display so the menu bar and Dock live on the bigger screen.

## Is one monitor the right setup for you?

A single monitor beside the laptop suits most people. If you need three apps side by side, an ultrawide may suit you better; if you move around a lot, multiple desktops on the laptop alone may be enough.
