To auto-hide the Dock on a Mac, open **System Settings → Desktop & Dock** and turn on **Automatically hide and show the Dock**, or press **Option-Command-D** to toggle it instantly. The Dock then disappears until you move the pointer to the edge of the screen, and windows can use the full height of the display.

On a laptop, this is one of the simplest ways to get more room for windows. Here is how to set it up and what else helps.

## How do I hide the Dock?

**With the keyboard:** press <kbd>Option</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>D</kbd>. Apple also lists <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>A</kbd> for showing or hiding the Dock. Press again to switch back.

**In settings:**

1. Choose **Apple menu → System Settings**.
2. Click **Desktop & Dock** in the sidebar.
3. Turn on **Automatically hide and show the Dock**.

To bring the Dock back while it is hidden, move the pointer to the bottom of the screen (or the side, if the Dock is on the left or right).

## How much space does hiding the Dock give me?

It depends on the Dock size setting, but the Dock always takes a strip along one edge when shown. Every filled or tiled window stops short of it, and on a laptop screen with well under 1,000 points of usable height, that strip is a noticeable share. Hidden, that space goes to your windows.

The [window size calculator](/tools/window-size-calculator/) has a field for the Dock: enter its height to see window sizes with it shown, then set it to zero to see the gain.

## Should I move the Dock instead of hiding it?

If you like seeing the Dock, put it on the side. Use **Position on screen** in Desktop & Dock settings to choose **Left** or **Right**. Most Mac screens are much wider than they are tall, so giving up some width costs less than giving up height. This works well on wide monitors in particular.

Other Dock settings that save space or clutter:

| Setting | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Size** | A smaller Dock takes less space when shown |
| **Magnification** | Icons grow only under the pointer, so the Dock can stay small |
| **Minimize windows into application icon** | Minimised windows do not add icons to the Dock |
| **Show suggested and recent apps in Dock** | Turn off to keep the Dock shorter |

## Does a hidden Dock get in the way?

Two things people notice:

- **Accidental reveals.** Moving the pointer to the bottom edge, for example to reach a scroll bar or a bottom-snapped window, can pop the Dock up over it. Putting the Dock on the side avoids this.
- **The Dock on multiple displays.** With **Displays have separate Spaces** on, Apple notes the Dock can appear on any display; with it off, the Dock stays on the main display.

## What else gives windows more space?

- **Hide the menu bar** as well: see [how to auto-hide the menu bar](/blog/auto-hide-menu-bar-mac/).
- **Use Fill instead of full screen**, so windows take all the space while you keep the menu bar and other apps. See [maximising without full screen](/blog/maximize-window-mac-without-full-screen/).
- **Snap windows to exact layouts.** With a window manager such as [Windowmac](/), halves, thirds and quarters always fill the usable area exactly, Dock or not, and you can set a gap between windows if you like some breathing room.

## Which setup should I choose?

| Screen | Suggested Dock setup |
| --- | --- |
| 13 or 14-inch laptop | Auto-hide, bottom |
| Laptop with a wide external monitor | On the side, or auto-hide |
| Ultrawide monitor | Left or right, shown |
| You rely on Dock badges | Shown, small, on the side |
