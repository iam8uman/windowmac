To use multiple desktops on a Mac, open **Mission Control** (Control-Up Arrow or a three-finger swipe up), move the pointer to the top of the screen and click the **+** button to add a desktop, which macOS calls a space. Switch between spaces with **Control-Left Arrow** and **Control-Right Arrow**, or swipe sideways with three or four fingers. You can have up to 16 spaces.

Spaces work best when each one has a job. Here is how to set them up, move things between them and stop them shuffling around.

## How do I create and delete desktops?

**To add a space:**

1. Open Mission Control with <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>↑</kbd>.
2. Move the pointer to the top edge to open the **Spaces bar**.
3. Click the **Add (+)** button at the right end.

**To delete a space:** in the same Spaces bar, hover over its thumbnail and click the **Delete** button that appears. Its windows move to another space rather than closing.

Full-screen apps and Split View pairs also appear in the Spaces bar. They are spaces too, which is why a full-screen app seems to "disappear" from your desktop: it has moved to a space of its own.

## How do I switch between desktops?

| How | Move left or right |
| --- | --- |
| Keyboard | <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>←</kbd> or <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>→</kbd> |
| Trackpad | Swipe left or right with three or four fingers |
| Magic Mouse | Swipe left or right with two fingers |
| Mission Control | Click a space in the Spaces bar |

## How do I move a window to another desktop?

Two ways, both built in:

- **Through Mission Control.** Open it, then drag the window up onto the space you want in the Spaces bar.
- **By dragging to the edge.** Drag the window by its title bar against the left or right edge of the screen and hold it there. After a moment it moves to the next space.

On macOS Sequoia 15 and later the left and right edges are also tiling zones. If the window tiles instead of moving to the next space, use the Mission Control method.

## How do I keep an app on one desktop?

You can pin an app so it always opens in a particular space:

1. Go to the space you want the app on.
2. Control-click the app's icon in the **Dock** (open the app first if it is not there).
3. Choose **Options**, then under **Assign To** pick one of:
   - **This Desktop:** the app always opens here.
   - **All Desktops:** the app appears on every space, useful for a music player or chat app.
   - **Desktop on Display**: the app opens on a specific monitor (needs more than one display).
   - **None:** the app opens wherever you are.

## Which Spaces settings should I change?

In **System Settings → Desktop & Dock**, under **Mission Control**:

- **Automatically rearrange Spaces based on most recent use.** Turn this off. With it on, the order of your spaces changes as you use them, which breaks the habit of "email is always on the left".
- **When switching to an application, switch to a Space with open windows for the application.** Keep it on if you sort apps by space, so Command-Tab takes you to the right one. Turn it off if you would rather bring the app to your current space.
- **Displays have separate Spaces.** With more than one monitor, this gives each display its own set of spaces. It is also needed for Stage Manager.

## How should I organise my desktops?

A few patterns that work for many people:

| Space | What lives there |
| --- | --- |
| 1: Communication | Mail, calendar, chat |
| 2: Main work | Editor, browser for research |
| 3: Reference | Documentation, notes, files |
| 4: Personal | Music, personal browser profile |

Inside each space, windows still need arranging. Tiling two windows side by side, or putting three in thirds on a wide monitor, keeps each space ready to work in. ## Spaces, Stage Manager or full screen?

| You want | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Separate contexts you swipe between | Desktop spaces |
| One screen, recent apps parked at the side | [Stage Manager](/blog/stage-manager-mac/) |
| One or two apps, no distractions | Full screen or Split View |
| A bird's-eye view to find a window | [Mission Control](/blog/mission-control-mac/) |

If you are unsure which suits you, the [Mac multitasking style quiz](/quiz/mac-multitasking-style/) asks seven questions and points you to the right one.
