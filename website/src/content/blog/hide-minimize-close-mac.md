On a Mac, **hiding** an app (Command-H) makes all its windows disappear until you switch back to it; **minimising** a window (Command-M) shrinks that one window into the Dock; **closing** a window (Command-W) removes it, but the app usually keeps running; and **quitting** (Command-Q) ends the app. For clearing clutter, hiding is usually best, because every window comes back exactly as it was with one click or Command-Tab.

Windows users often reach for minimise, since that is the habit on a PC. On a Mac, the other options are often faster. Here is how each behaves.

## How do hide, minimise, close and quit compare?

| | Hide | Minimise | Close | Quit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shortcut | <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>H</kbd> | <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>M</kbd> | <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>W</kbd> | <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Q</kbd> |
| Affects | Every window of the app | One window | One window | The whole app |
| App still running | Yes | Yes | Usually | No |
| Where it goes | Nowhere visible | Into the Dock | Gone | Gone |
| How to get it back | Switch to the app | Click it in the Dock | Reopen the document or a new window | Open the app again |

## What does hiding do?

Hiding removes all of an app's windows from the screen at once. The app stays running and its Dock icon stays where it is. Switch back with <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Tab</kbd> or by clicking its icon, and every window returns to its old size and position.

Two shortcuts make hiding useful:

- <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>H</kbd> hides the app you are in.
- <kbd>Option</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>H</kbd> hides every other app, which is the quickest way to focus on one thing.

## What does minimising do?

Minimising shrinks one window into the Dock. It stays there until you click it, which makes minimised windows easy to lose track of when several pile up.

Useful variations:

- <kbd>Option</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>M</kbd> minimises every window of the front app.
- Double-clicking a title bar can minimise, if you set **double-clicking a window's title bar** to Minimize in **Desktop & Dock** settings.
- Turn on **Minimize windows into application icon** in the same settings to stop minimised windows filling up the right side of the Dock. They then hide inside their app's icon instead.

## What does closing do?

Closing removes one window. In most apps the app keeps running even when its last window closes, which is why a dot stays under its Dock icon. Apple notes that <kbd>Option</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>W</kbd> closes all of an app's windows while the app stays open.

Some single-window apps do quit when you close their window, but that is the app's choice, not the Mac's rule.

## What does quitting do?

<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Q</kbd> ends the app and closes all its windows. Whether those windows come back next time depends on the app and on the **Close windows when quitting an application** setting in **Desktop & Dock**: with it on, apps start fresh. If an app stops responding, <kbd>Option</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Esc</kbd> opens Force Quit.

## Which should I use?

| You want to | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Clear an app away but come back soon | Hide (Command-H) |
| Focus on one app right now | Hide others (Option-Command-H) |
| Park one window out of several | Minimise (Command-M), or move it to another desktop |
| Finish with a document | Close (Command-W) |
| Free up memory or restart an app | Quit (Command-Q) |

If you minimise because windows overlap and get in the way, arranging them may solve the problem better. [Snapping windows](/blog/how-to-snap-windows-on-mac/) puts them side by side, and [Windowmac](/) adds a **Stash** command (hold Option and press the comma or full stop key) that tucks a window behind the screen edge with a sliver showing, so it is out of the way but one push of the pointer from coming back.
