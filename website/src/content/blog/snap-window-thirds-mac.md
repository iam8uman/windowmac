macOS cannot snap a window to a third of the screen: its built-in tiling only does halves and quarters. To get thirds, install a window manager. With the free **Windowmac**, hold **Option** and press an arrow key twice for a third, or **Option-T** for the centre third. **Rectangle**, **Loop** and **Raycast** also offer thirds for free.

Thirds matter most on wide screens, where a half is wider than most apps need. Here is how to get them in each app, including two thirds and vertical thirds.

## Why does macOS not have thirds?

Apple's window tiling, added in macOS Sequoia 15, lists left, right, top and bottom halves, the four quarters, fill, centre and several two- and three-window arrangements. There is no third or two-thirds layout in the green button menu, the Window menu or the tiling shortcuts. You can see the full built-in list in our guide to [macOS window tiling shortcuts](/blog/macos-window-tiling-shortcuts/).

## Which thirds layouts are there?

| Layout | Width | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left, centre or right third | One third | Chat, notes, terminal, reference |
| Left or right two thirds | Two thirds | Main app next to a third |
| Top, middle or bottom third | Full width, one third high | Vertical monitors |
| Top or bottom two thirds | Full width, two thirds high | Vertical monitors |

## How do I snap to thirds with Windowmac?

[Windowmac](/) is free and open source and runs on macOS 13 or later. Its arrow keys cycle through sizes, so thirds are one extra press away from halves.

**With the keyboard (hold Option the whole time):**

1. Press <kbd>←</kbd> once for the left half, again for the **left third**, again for the **left two thirds**.
2. Press <kbd>→</kbd> the same way for the right side.
3. Press <kbd>T</kbd> for the **centre third**.

**With the mouse:**

1. Hold **Option**. A ring appears under the pointer.
2. Flick left or right. The outline shows a half.
3. Click while still holding Option to step to a third, then two thirds.
4. Release to apply.

**Vertical thirds** (top, middle and bottom, and top or bottom two thirds) are built into Windowmac too. They are not on a default key, so assign them in Settings to whichever keys or ring directions you like. On a portrait monitor, putting top and bottom thirds on the up and down ring directions makes them a single flick.

## How do I get thirds in other free apps?

- **Rectangle.** Drag a window to the screen edges and corners for halves, quarters and thirds, or use its keyboard shortcuts, which also include first, centre and last thirds and even sixths and ninths.
- **Loop.** A radial window manager like Windowmac with horizontal and vertical thirds on keys paired with its trigger key.
- **Raycast.** Its free window management commands include First Third, Center Third, Last Third and the two-thirds versions. Assign each a hotkey in Raycast's settings.

Pick by how you like to move windows: Rectangle if you drag to edges, Raycast if you already use it as a launcher, Windowmac or Loop if you want a gesture under the pointer.

## When are thirds the right choice?

Thirds suit any screen where half is too wide. A rough guide, using each screen's looks-like width:

| Display (looks like) | Third width | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 13 to 14-inch laptop (about 1,470 to 1,512 points) | About 490 to 504 points | Narrow, use two thirds plus one third |
| 27-inch at 2,560 points | About 853 points | Comfortable |
| 34-inch ultrawide, 3,440 points | About 1,147 points | Ideal |
| 49-inch super ultrawide, 5,120 points | About 1,707 points | Consider quarters too |

For any monitor, the [ultrawide split calculator](/tools/ultrawide-split-calculator/) shows the exact width of each column in points and centimetres, and our [ultrawide layouts guide](/blog/ultrawide-monitor-mac-layouts/) covers the best arrangements for very wide screens.

## Which way should I get thirds?

| You | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Use the mouse on a big monitor | Windowmac or Loop |
| Drag windows to edges | Rectangle |
| Already use Raycast | Raycast window commands |
| Have a vertical monitor | An app with vertical thirds, such as Windowmac or Loop |
