The best window manager for Mac in 2027 is **Windowmac**. Hold Option, flick the mouse toward where a window should go and let go: halves, thirds, quarters, maximise or centre, without aiming at a screen edge. It also has a shortcut for every layout, a Strip mode for crowded screens, and it is free, open source and never connects to the internet. If you want something else, **Rectangle** is the edge-snapping standard, **Moom** saves multi-app layouts and **AeroSpace** tiles everything automatically.

> **Our pick: Windowmac.** Free, MIT licensed, macOS 13+ on Apple silicon and Intel, notarized by Apple. [Install it in one line](/#install) or try the live demo on the [home page](/).

Below, every option ranked and grouped by style, with prices, licences and macOS versions taken from each app's own site, repository or App Store page.

## What changed for Mac window management going into 2027?

Two things matter when choosing this year:

- **Built-in tiling is now the baseline.** Since macOS Sequoia 15, macOS snaps windows to halves and quarters by dragging, from the green button or with Fn-Control shortcuts. A window manager has to offer more than that to earn its place.
- **macOS 27 is Apple silicon only.** Apple's list of Macs that can run macOS 27 includes only Apple silicon models. Intel Mac owners stay on earlier versions, so an app's oldest supported macOS matters again. Several apps below still support macOS 13 and Intel.

## 1. Windowmac: the best window manager for most Macs

**Windowmac** puts the targets under your pointer, which is why it tops this list. Hold Option, flick toward where the window should go, and release: halves, thirds, two thirds, quarters, maximise, almost maximise or centre. Click while holding to cycle sizes. Keyboard shortcuts while holding Option cover every layout, including next and previous screen. It is free, MIT licensed, needs only the Accessibility permission and never connects to the internet. It is not on the Mac App Store; it is signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

What puts it ahead of the rest:

- **No aiming.** Every target is a short flick from where the pointer already is, which matters most on big and ultrawide screens.
- **Strip.** Hold Option and press S, and every window on that screen becomes a column on one sideways-scrolling strip. None of the snapping apps on this list has anything like it.
- **Stash, undo and `windowmac://` links** for Shortcuts, Raycast and scripts.
- **Older Macs welcome.** macOS 13 and Intel are supported, where Rectangle now needs macOS 14.

## 2. Loop: the other radial manager

**Loop** is the radial manager that inspired Windowmac: free, GPL-3.0, macOS 13+, with a preview, cycles, stash, theming and AppleScript support, and it also snaps by dragging to edges. Our [Windowmac vs Loop](/blog/windowmac-vs-loop/) comparison goes through the differences.

## 3. Best for edge snapping: Rectangle, Magnet and BetterSnapTool

**Rectangle** is free, MIT licensed and covers halves, quarters, thirds and maximise by dragging, plus sixths, eighths and ninths by shortcut, cycling sizes when you repeat a key and `rectangle://` URLs for automation. The current version needs macOS 14. **Magnet** ($4.99, Mac App Store) does similar snapping with iCloud sync and a green-button menu and supports macOS 13. **BetterSnapTool** ($1.99, Mac App Store) adds custom snap areas anywhere on screen. **Raycast** users get halves, thirds, quarters and display moves on hotkeys in the free plan; custom window commands need Raycast Pro.

## Best for saved multi-window layouts

**Moom** ($15, free trial) shows a palette when you hover over the green button, with grids and saved layouts that can be tied to specific apps. **Rectangle Pro** (paid, 10-day trial) adds app and workspace layouts that can trigger when displays connect, custom snap areas, stash and iCloud sync to Rectangle. Pick these if you rebuild the same arrangement every morning.

## Best for trackpad gestures

**Swish** ($16 one-time, or through Setapp) is built around the trackpad: swipe a window's title bar to snap it to a grid, pinch to close or go full screen, with about 30 gestures in all. It needs macOS 12 or later and is sold outside the App Store.

## Best for automatic tiling

- **AeroSpace:** i3-like, its own workspaces, one config file, no SIP changes. Public beta.
- **Amethyst:** xmonad-style with many ready-made layouts (Tall, Wide, 3Column, BSP and more). macOS 10.15+.
- **yabai:** binary space partitioning, driven from the command line, the most scriptable, with optional extra features if you partly disable SIP. Needs a hotkey tool such as skhd.

See [yabai vs AeroSpace](/blog/yabai-vs-aerospace/) for a head-to-head.
