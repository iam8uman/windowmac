To show the desktop on a Mac, press **Command-Mission Control**, **Fn-H** or **Fn-F11**, or spread your thumb and three fingers apart on the trackpad. Every window slides to the edges of the screen so you can see the files and widgets on your desktop. Do the same again, or pinch, to bring the windows back exactly where they were.

There are a few more ways, and one setting that explains why windows sometimes vanish when you click the wallpaper.

## What are the ways to show the desktop?

| Method | How | Bring windows back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keyboard | <kbd>Command</kbd>-Mission Control, <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>H</kbd> or <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>F11</kbd> | Same keys again |
| Trackpad | Spread thumb and three fingers | Pinch them together |
| Wallpaper click | Click an empty part of the desktop (if enabled) | Click the wallpaper again |
| Hot corner | Push the pointer into a corner set to Desktop | Push it in again |

All of these are temporary. Nothing is closed, hidden or minimised; the windows just move aside.

## How do I show the desktop with the keyboard?

Apple lists three shortcuts that do the same job:

- <kbd>Command</kbd>-Mission Control
- <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>H</kbd>
- <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>F11</kbd>

<kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>H</kbd> is usually the easiest to reach. On keyboards with a globe key, that key is Fn. If none of them work, check **System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Shortcuts**, where you can see or change the Show Desktop key.

## How do I show the desktop by clicking the wallpaper?

macOS can clear windows away when you click the wallpaper. It is controlled by the **Show Desktop** option in **System Settings → Desktop & Dock**, under Desktop & Stage Manager:

- **Always on Wallpaper Click:** any click on the wallpaper moves windows aside to show desktop items and widgets.
- **Only in Stage Manager on Click:** the same, but only while Stage Manager is on.

If your windows keep disappearing when you click between them, this is the cause. Set it to **Only in Stage Manager**.

## How do I set a hot corner for the desktop?

1. Open **System Settings → Desktop & Dock** and click **Hot Corners**.
2. Pick a corner and choose **Desktop** from its menu.
3. Click **Done**.

To avoid triggering it by accident, hold a modifier key such as Command while choosing the action; then the corner only works when you hold that key. Our [hot corners guide](/blog/hot-corners-mac/) covers the other actions you can put on corners.

## What is the quickest way to drop a file on the desktop?

Apple suggests this trick: start dragging the file from its window, press <kbd>Command</kbd>-Mission Control to show the desktop, drop the file, then press the keys again to bring your windows back. It also works for copying: copy the item, show the desktop, paste, and restore.

## How is this different from minimising or hiding?

| Action | What happens | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Show Desktop | All windows move aside temporarily | A quick look at desktop files or widgets |
| Hide (Command-H) | One app's windows vanish until you return to it | Clearing one app away |
| Hide others (Option-Command-H) | Every app except the front one hides | Focusing on one app |
| Minimise (Command-M) | One window goes to the Dock | Parking a window for later |

The full differences are explained in [hide vs minimise vs close](/blog/hide-minimize-close-mac/).

## Why does Show Desktop not work in full screen?

Full-screen apps live in their own space, so there are no other windows to move aside and no desktop behind them. Leave full screen first with Control-Command-F, or swipe to a normal desktop space, then show the desktop. Our guide to [exiting full screen](/blog/exit-full-screen-mac/) covers every way out.

## What if I want windows out of the way more permanently?

Show Desktop is for a quick glance. If you constantly fight with windows covering each other, arranging them is a better fix than clearing them. [Snapping windows](/blog/how-to-snap-windows-on-mac/) puts two or more side by side with no overlap, and [Windowmac](/) can tuck a window behind the screen edge with **Stash** (hold Option and press the comma or full stop key), leaving a sliver you push against to slide it back out.
