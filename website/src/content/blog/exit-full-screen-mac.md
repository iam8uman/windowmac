To exit full screen on a Mac, press **Control-Command-F**. You can also move the pointer to the top of the screen so the title bar appears, then hover over the green button and choose **Exit Full Screen**. Either way the app goes back to a normal window on your desktop, with the menu bar and Dock visible again.

If those do not work, or you are in Split View rather than plain full screen, the steps below cover every case.

## How do I exit full screen with the keyboard?

Press <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>F</kbd>. Apple lists it as the shortcut to enter or exit full screen in apps that support it, so the same keys take you in and out.

In many apps <kbd>Esc</kbd> also works. Apple's getting-started guide for new Mac users says pressing Esc returns a full-screen window to its previous size. Some apps use Esc for something else, such as cancelling a dialog, so Control-Command-F is the more reliable choice.

## How do I exit full screen with the mouse?

1. Move the pointer to the very top of the screen. The menu bar and the window's title bar slide into view.
2. Hover over the **green button** in the top-left corner of the window.
3. Choose **Exit Full Screen** from the menu that appears.

Clicking the green button directly also leaves full screen, because it toggles the mode. If you prefer menus, choose **Window → Full Screen Tile → Exit Full Screen** from the menu bar while the app is active.

## How do I exit full screen from Mission Control?

This works even when an app is not responding to clicks.

1. Press <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>↑</kbd>, or swipe up with three or four fingers, to open Mission Control.
2. Move the pointer to the top edge so the **Spaces bar** opens.
3. Hover over the full-screen app's thumbnail and click the **Exit** button that appears on it.

The app returns to your normal desktop. This is also the quickest way to close several full-screen spaces in a row.

## How do I get out of Split View?

Split View is two apps sharing full screen, so the steps are a little different.

1. Click one of the two apps.
2. Hover over its green button.
3. Choose **Move Window to Desktop**.

That app goes back to your desktop, and the other app stays full screen in its own space. Repeat for the second app, or use any of the methods above to exit its full screen.

## What if the menu bar won't appear in full screen?

The menu bar is hidden in full screen until you move the pointer to the top edge. If it still will not show:

- **Move the pointer right to the top edge** and hold it there for a moment.
- **Use the keyboard** instead: Control-Command-F does not need the menu bar.
- **Keep the menu bar on permanently.** Open **System Settings → Menu Bar** and set **Automatically hide and show the menu bar** to **Never**. Apple notes that this keeps it visible in full screen too.

If the whole app is frozen, force quit it with <kbd>Option</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Esc</kbd>.

## How do I stop ending up in full screen by accident?

Most accidental full screen comes from clicking the green button when you meant to make a window bigger. The habits that avoid it:

- **Use Fill instead.** Press <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>F</kbd> or choose **Window → Fill** on macOS Sequoia 15 or later. The window fills the desktop but stays on it. Our guide to [maximising without full screen](/blog/maximize-window-mac-without-full-screen/) has every option.
- **Change the title bar double-click** to Fill in **Desktop & Dock** settings, so double-clicking is a safe way to enlarge a window.
- **Use a window manager** for sizing. In [Windowmac](/), holding Option and flicking up maximises the window on the normal desktop, so the green button is no longer part of your routine.

## When is full screen actually useful?

Full screen is good for one job at a time: a presentation, a film, or writing without distractions on a small laptop. When you want two apps and no distractions, Split View is the full-screen version of [splitting your screen](/blog/how-to-split-screen-on-mac/). When you want to see several windows and still reach the menu bar and Dock, tiling is the better fit.

| You want | Use |
| --- | --- |
| No menu bar or Dock, one app | Full screen |
| No menu bar or Dock, two apps | Split View |
| Big window, keep everything else | Fill (Fn-Control-F) |
| Several windows arranged neatly | Tiling or a window manager |
