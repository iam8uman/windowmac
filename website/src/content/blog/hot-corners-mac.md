To set up Hot Corners on a Mac, open **System Settings → Desktop & Dock**, scroll down and click **Hot Corners**, then choose an action from the menu for each corner and click **Done**. After that, pushing the pointer into that corner of the screen runs the action, such as opening Mission Control, showing the desktop or locking the screen.

Hot Corners are one of the fastest mouse-only shortcuts on a Mac, and also one of the easiest to trigger by accident. Here is how to set them up so they help.

## How do I set up Hot Corners?

1. Choose **Apple menu → System Settings**.
2. Click **Desktop & Dock** in the sidebar (you may need to scroll).
3. Scroll to the bottom and click **Hot Corners**.
4. For each corner, open its menu and choose an action.
5. Click **Done**.

The bottom-right corner is set to **Quick Note** by default, which is why a note sometimes pops up when you push the pointer there.

## Which actions can a hot corner run?

The menu includes window and screen actions such as **Mission Control**, **Application Windows**, **Desktop**, **Notification Center**, **Quick Note**, **Start Screen Saver**, **Put Display to Sleep** and **Lock Screen**. The exact list depends on your macOS version. For window management, the useful ones are:

| Action | What it does | Keyboard equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission Control | Shows every window and space | <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>↑</kbd> |
| Application Windows | Shows only the current app's windows | <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>↓</kbd> |
| Desktop | Moves all windows aside | <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>F11</kbd> |
| Lock Screen | Locks the Mac | <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Q</kbd> |

Hot Corners shine when your hand is already on the mouse. If you live on the keyboard, the shortcuts in the last column are quicker. Our [Mission Control guide](/blog/mission-control-mac/) covers what each view shows.

## How do I stop Hot Corners firing by accident?

Add a modifier key. While a corner's menu is open, hold **Command**, **Shift**, **Option** or **Control** (or a combination) and then choose the action. The corner then only works while you hold that key.

This is worth doing for anything disruptive, such as Lock Screen or Put Display to Sleep, and for corners you pass through often. The top-left corner is near the Apple menu, and the top-right is near Control Center, so plain hot corners there get triggered a lot.

## How do I turn off a hot corner?

Open the corner's menu and choose the dash at the top, which means no action. Do this for the Quick Note corner if you never use it.

## Which corners should I use for what?

A setup that suits many window-heavy workflows:

| Corner | Action | Modifier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top left | Mission Control | None |
| Top right | Notification Center | None |
| Bottom left | Desktop | None |
| Bottom right | Lock Screen | Command |

Pick corners away from where you click most. If your Dock sits on the left or right, avoid putting a frequent action in a corner next to it.

## Can I use Hot Corners with Stage Manager or Spaces?

Yes. Hot Corners run the same actions whatever else is on, so a Mission Control corner is a quick way to see your spaces, and a Desktop corner clears Stage Manager's windows aside too. If you use a hot corner to open Mission Control, you can drag windows between spaces from there; our [multiple desktops guide](/blog/multiple-desktops-mac/) covers that.

## Hot Corners on multiple monitors

The corners are the outer corners of your whole display arrangement. Where two displays meet, the pointer slides across to the other screen instead of stopping, so those inside corners are hard to hit. If you rearrange your displays, check that the corners you rely on are still on an outside edge. Our [multi-monitor planner](/tools/multi-monitor-planner/) can help you see how your displays line up.

## Are there better tools for window jobs?

Hot Corners are great for one-off views like Mission Control. For actually placing windows, they do nothing; snapping is the next step. [How to snap windows on a Mac](/blog/how-to-snap-windows-on-mac/) covers the built-in way, and [Windowmac](/) gives you a ring under the pointer when you hold Option, so placing a window becomes the same kind of quick flick as a hot corner, without travelling to the corner first.
