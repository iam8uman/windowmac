To split screen on a Mac, drag a window to the left or right edge of the screen until a highlighted area appears, then let go; do the same with a second window on the other side. You can also hover over the green button in a window's top-left corner and pick a tiling layout, press **Fn-Control-Left Arrow** or **Fn-Control-Right Arrow**, or use a free window manager such as [Windowmac](/) to do it with one flick of the mouse.

There are four ways in total. They differ in speed and in whether your menu bar and Dock stay visible, so here is each one, then a quick way to choose.

## How do I split screen by dragging a window?

Recent versions of macOS have window tiling built in. It is the quickest method that needs nothing installed.

1. Drag the first window by its title bar to the **left or right edge** of the screen.
2. When a highlighted area appears, release the window. It fills that half.
3. Drag the second window to the opposite edge and release.

Drag to a **corner** to get a quarter of the screen, or to the **menu bar** to fill the whole desktop. If you hold **Option** while dragging, the highlighted areas appear sooner, so you do not need to reach all the way to the edge.

If nothing happens, tiling may be off. Open **System Settings → Desktop & Dock**, scroll to **Windows**, and check the switches for dragging windows to screen edges to tile.

## How do I use the green button to split screen?

Every standard Mac window has a green button in its top-left corner. Hover over it (do not click) and a menu appears.

- **Move & Resize** tiles the window on your normal desktop: left, right, top, bottom or a quarter.
- **Fill & Arrange** places two or more front windows at once, for example left and right.
- **Full Screen Tile → Left of Screen** or **Right of Screen** starts **Split View**, described below.

The same choices are in the menu bar under **Window → Move & Resize**, which is handy when a window's buttons are off-screen.

## What are the keyboard shortcuts for split screen on Mac?

These shortcuts come from Apple's window tiling list. They act on the window you are using.

| Shortcut | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>←</kbd> | Move the window to the left half |
| <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>→</kbd> | Move the window to the right half |
| <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>↑</kbd> / <kbd>↓</kbd> | Top half / bottom half |
| <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>F</kbd> | Fill the screen without going full screen |
| <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>C</kbd> | Centre the window |
| <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>R</kbd> | Return to the previous size |
| <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>←</kbd> | Arrange the two front windows left and right |
| <kbd>Fn</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>↑</kbd> | Arrange the two front windows top and bottom |

On many keyboards the **Fn** key is also the **Globe** key. There are no built-in shortcuts for the four quarters, and none for thirds. For a full list, see our [Mac window keyboard shortcuts](/blog/topics/keyboard-shortcuts/) guides.

## What is Split View, and when should I use it?

Split View puts two apps side by side in **full screen**, in a desktop space of their own. The menu bar and Dock hide until you move the pointer to them, which removes distractions on a small laptop screen.

1. Hover over the green button and choose **Full Screen Tile → Left of Screen** (or **Right of Screen**).
2. On the other side, click the second app you want.
3. Drag the divider in the middle to change the split. Double-click it to reset.

To swap sides, drag one window by its title bar to the other side. To leave, hover over the green button and choose **Move Window to Desktop**.

Split View has limits. Not every app supports it, it holds only two apps, and switching to anything else means leaving the space. For everyday multitasking, tiling on the normal desktop is usually easier.

## How do I split screen with one flick?

The built-in methods need either a careful drag to the edge or a three-key shortcut. A window manager gives you a faster gesture plus layouts macOS does not have, such as **thirds** and **two thirds**.

With [Windowmac](/), a free and open-source window manager:

1. Hold **Option**. A small ring appears right under your pointer.
2. Nudge the mouse toward where the window should go: left, right, a corner or up to maximise.
3. Let go. The window snaps into place.

Click while holding to cycle between half, third and two thirds. While Option is held, the arrow keys do the same from the keyboard. Windowmac can also send a window to your other display with its layout intact, which helps if you work with more than one monitor.

To see exactly how big each half, third or quarter will be on your display, try the free [window size calculator](/tools/window-size-calculator/).

## Which split screen method should I use?

| You want to | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Put two windows side by side with nothing installed | Drag to the edge, or Fn-Control-Arrow |
| Focus on two apps with no menu bar or Dock | Split View |
| Arrange two windows in one keystroke | Fn-Control-Shift-Arrow |
| Use thirds on a wide or ultrawide display | A window manager such as Windowmac |
| Snap windows without aiming at an edge | A gesture window manager such as Windowmac |
